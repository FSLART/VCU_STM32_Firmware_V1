T26e Traction Control Rework

The traction control now works from the derivative of each rear motor's rpm, compared with what the car can actually do. A rear wheel that speeds up faster than the car can accelerate is spinning, and gets its torque cut. It no longer needs the front wheel speeds, which read zero on track. The values come from the 08-10 ESTG log and a model of the car. It compiles and links, and has not been on the car yet.

Why the old traction control never acted

The old controller was a PI per rear wheel that compared it with the front wheel on the same side. The front wheel sensors (AQT2) read 0 above about 4 km/h, a hardware fault the 08-10 and 10-10 reports traced to the input stage. With the reference at zero, the controller was off almost all the time. At Maceira on 10-10 it cut torque for 1.4 s in the whole day, always near standstill.

An earlier attempt without the front wheels ("version E", 08-10) compared both rear wheels with the slower one, allowed to rise at 15 km/h per second (0.42 g). It cut the outer wheel in every corner and held the car under 29 km/h. The new design avoids both problems: each wheel is judged only against its own past, and the acceleration allowed is what the car really does.

How it works

Each control step (every 5 ms in ready-to-drive), for each rear wheel:

  1. Detect a new ERPM frame. The inverters send ERPM only every 32.8 ms, so between frames the value stands still. A change in the ERPM value marks a new frame. While driving, only 0.2 % of frames repeat the previous value, and the next frame then spans both.
  2. Move the reference speed, once per frame: it follows the wheel, but rises no faster than the car limit and falls no faster than the car can brake, over the time since the last frame.
  3. Compute the wheel acceleration, once per frame: the speed change since the newest frame at least 55 ms older (two frames).
  4. Cut torque with three terms, and send the request times the torque factor to that motor.
// traction_control_torque_vectoring.c, per rear wheel car limit = min(MAXIMUM_ACCELERATION_MS2, MAXIMUM_POWER_W_PER_KG / reference speed) 10 m/s², 200 W/kg reference speed = clamp(wheel speed, ref − 15 m/s² · Δt_frame, ref + car limit · Δt_frame) wheel acceleration = Δ wheel speed / Δt over ≥ DERIVATIVE_WINDOW_MS (55 ms = 2 frames) acceleration excess = wheel acceleration − car limit − ACCELERATION_MARGIN_MS2 > 0: spinning up speed error = wheel speed − (reference · (1 + SLIP_TARGET) + SPEED_MARGIN_KMH) > 0: spun up integral term += INTEGRAL_GAIN · speed error · dt while speed error > 0 −= RECOVERY_PER_S · dt otherwise, within [0, 0.8] torque factor = 1 − ACCELERATION_GAIN · max(acceleration excess, 0) − PROPORTIONAL_GAIN · max(speed error, 0) − integral term, within [0.2, 1]

The acceleration term reacts as the wheel starts to spin, before slip builds up. The proportional term holds the cut until the wheel is back with the car. The integral term remembers how much torque this surface takes and gives it back slowly. After a wheel lock under regen, the wheel catching back up with the car is not treated as spin for one window.

What the logs set

The 08-10 ESTG night is the only log with the full-rate powertrain bus (Vector GL2400, 31 min moving, 2557 s in ready-to-drive). Car speed was rebuilt the way the team's reports do it: the mean of both rears where both roll with the car, interpolated through spins and locks.

ERPM arrives every 32.8 ms (99.8 % of frames under 40 ms while moving, p99 33.6 ms). That is about 30 Hz, the same as the 28 Hz seen in the 10-10 ROS bags. A derivative over one 5 ms step would read zero, then 6–7 times the real value. Over one frame, the driveline ripple of a gripping wheel reads up to 17 m/s². Over two frames it stays under 11.3 m/s², clear of the 13 m/s² where the cut starts.

Gripping, the car never passed 8.8 m/s² (Maceira 10-10: 6.3 at p99). At 60–80 km/h it reaches about 160 W/kg, the inverters' power limit (2 × 32 kW, 76 % to the wheels, 290 kg). The TC limit sits above both: 10 m/s², and 200 W/kg above 72 km/h.

Replayed on the 08-10 night

A Python twin of the firmware code (same order, same equations) ran open loop over the whole log, on the ERPM the VCU would have seen. That night the TC was off or blind, so the wheels did what they did, and the replay shows where the new code would have cut.

  • 0 of 555 cut onsets happened while both rears gripped and the wheel was within 1 km/h of the car.
  • 203 of 219 spins cut (93 %). The first cut came at a median 2.9 km/h of slip, 8.4 km/h at p90.
  • With a 1-frame window, the time spent cutting gripping wheels over the night went from 0.5 s to 2.8 s.

Choosing the gains

How much to cut and how fast to give torque back cannot be read from open-loop logs, so the gains came from a straight-line model of the car built with its numbers. Car: 290 kg, 55 % rear, CG 0.35 m high. Motor: 0.338·iq − 1.99 N·m (02-10 dyno), 120 Apk, 32 kW per inverter, 14.73 gear. Tyre peak at slip 0.10. The VCU and ERPM timing are the real ones (5 ms step, 32.8 ms frames). Every setting was scored over five plants (grip μ 0.8, 1.05 and 1.3; wheel inertia 0.15 to 0.3 kg·m²) and three cases (full pedal from 0, 30 and 60 km/h), against the best fixed pedal for each.

With μ 1.05 the best fixed pedal is 45 %, the same as the 35–45 % the Maceira report found the tyres take.

On low grip (μ 0.8) slip still reaches 15–28 km/h from a rolling start. The car can only do about 4–5 m/s² there, while the reference rises at the 10 m/s² limit, so a slowly creeping spin stays under it. That is the cost of having no reference speed sensor. On a slippery day, lower the car limit.

What changed

Only the VCU firmware repo. Committed as 9d2d890 “claude code for TC”.

  • Core/Src/traction_control_torque_vectoring.cNew controller: frame detection, reference speed, derivative, the three terms. Torque vectoring and sensor reading untouched.
  • Core/Inc/traction_control_torque_vectoring.hDescription and equations, parameters with where each value comes from, per-wheel state for Live Expressions, a stand test before the first run.
  • Core/Inc/throttle_control.hPipeline comment for step 8.
  • tools/throttle_map_editor.pyTraction tab lists the new settings. Its selftest finds all 57, none locked.
  • tools/vcu_live.pyAlso watches wheel acceleration, car limit and reference speeds.

Kept compatible: the tc_state values in VCU_states (0x750), so the DBC is unchanged; the function interface used by throttle_control.c.

Checks

  • Compiles with the project's ARM GCC 13.3 and flags, clean even with -Wextra -Wconversion -Wdouble-promotion.
  • The whole firmware links. The traction control state takes 348 bytes of RAM.
  • The generated Debug/ makefiles predate the traction control file, so a command-line make fails to link. CubeIDE regenerates them on its own build. The tracked Debug/VCU_STM32_Firmware_V1.elf that this deleted was restored from git.

Before and after the first run

  1. Stand test. Car on stands, press the pedal. The rear wheels should spin up in steps, on average at about 36 km/h per second, with traction_control.state at CUT_BOTH. Without TC they jump at once.
  2. On track, log tc_state and tc_factor_rear_left/right from VCU_states (0x750). With the debugger, watch acceleration and reference speed in vcu_live.py. Tune the gains from the editor.

Open points outside this change

  • Faster ERPM. Raising the inverters' ERPM message rate to 100 Hz or more (DTI configuration) would cut detection delay. The code adapts to faster frames by itself.
  • Throttle map speed. The map still uses the slower rear wheel as vehicle speed, so it asks for more torque when both rears spin. The TC fights this but does not remove it. The TC reference speed would be a better input.
  • Simulator twin. TractionControlTorqueVectoring.m still has the old controller. The header says so.
  • Regen locking. The 425 rear locks under regen at Maceira are not covered: regen does not go through the traction control. The same reference speed could drive an anti-lock on regen.
